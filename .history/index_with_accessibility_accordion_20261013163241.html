Building a More Accessible Community

At UCLA, accessibility is essential to creating a community where everyone feels welcome, respected, and empowered to participate fully.

Guided by our True Bruin Value of Belonging, we strive to build an inclusive environment that recognizes and respects the diverse identities, experiences, and abilities of our campus community. Accessibility goes beyond removing barriers and meeting legal requirements. It is about creating meaningful opportunities for everyone to learn, work, connect, and thrive.

Through our shared commitment to accessibility, we help build a UCLA where everyone has the opportunity to participate, contribute, and belong.

Understanding Accessibility Requirements

Accessibility laws, university policies, and technical standards help create equitable access to UCLA's programs, services, and digital experiences. Explore what each means, who it applies to, and why it matters.

Laws & Regulations

Federal and state laws establish accessibility rights and responsibilities for universities, including equal access to education, employment, programs, services, and digital environments.

Who they apply to: UCLA as a public university, and relevant departments, employees, programs, and services. Specific obligations vary by law and activity.

Why they matter: They protect people with disabilities from discrimination and help ensure meaningful access.

Examples include the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act.

UC & UCLA Policies

University of California and UCLA policies explain how accessibility commitments and applicable requirements are implemented across campus.

Who they apply to: The university community, including departments, faculty, staff, and other groups identified in each policy.

Why they matter: Policies clarify responsibilities, support consistent practices, and provide routes for assistance and accountability.

Consult the current official UC and UCLA policy documents to confirm the scope of each policy.

Accessibility Standards

Accessibility standards provide measurable criteria for creating and evaluating accessible websites, documents, applications, and physical spaces.

Who they apply to: Designers, developers, content creators, facilities teams, vendors, and others, depending on the standard and applicable requirement.

Why they matter: They translate accessibility goals into practical criteria that teams can design, test, and maintain.

For digital accessibility, the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are an important reference. The required version and conformance level depend on the applicable rule or policy.
